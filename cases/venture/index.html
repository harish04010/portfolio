AI agents for freight brokerages: great economics, unproven scale

The company

A seed-stage startup (a composite, not a specific firm) selling AI agents that do the repetitive work inside a freight brokerage: quoting loads, booking carriers, chasing tracking updates, and keeping shippers informed. It sells per operations seat, priced below the cost of the hours it saves.

Why it's plausible: the same workflow is built and tested in Waybill, a project in this portfolio, including the guardrails and human approval a broker would insist on.

Why now

  • Models can now do the work, not just draft it. Tool-using language models can read a rate confirmation, negotiate within limits and update a TMS, with code enforcing the limits.
  • Brokers are under margin pressure. Shippers put loads out to 5–10 brokers, compressing spreads on dry van to 10% or less [4]; labor is the cost line they can move.
  • The long tail is enormous. About 25,000 entities hold active broker authority, and most are small teams without in-house engineering [1].

Market size: why top-down numbers fail here

Published estimates of the US freight brokerage market disagree by more than 20×, because they measure different things.

The $160B figure is gross freight revenue flowing through brokers [2]; the $7.65B–$20.6B figures are research-firm estimates of brokerage revenue on narrower definitions [3][5]. None tells you what a broker would pay for software, so we size bottom-up instead.

Investment scorecard

Key risks

  • Feature, not company. If the TMS vendors ship good-enough agents, a standalone product gets squeezed.
  • Reliability. A booking error costs a broker real money and a carrier relationship. Guardrails and human approval are the product, not an add-on.
  • Inference costs. Gross margin depends on model prices; a 70% SaaS-like margin isn't guaranteed.
  • Freight cycles. When freight volumes fall, brokers cut seats first.

What diligence has to answer

  • Integration depth: does it write back into the top three TMS platforms, or just sit beside them?
  • Error rate in production: loads booked per human correction, measured on real customers.
  • Retention by cohort: do mid-size brokers keep their seats through a soft freight quarter?
  • Team: someone who has run a brokerage floor, plus engineers who've shipped agent systems. Unscored here because a composite has no team.

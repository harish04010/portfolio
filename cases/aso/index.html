The debate

What a bull has to believe

  • Comps turn sustainably positive. Q1 2026 comped +2.9% on traffic and ticket; eCommerce grew 12.8% in Q2 and 22.2% in Q3 2025.
  • New stores keep paying. They comped high single digits in Q3 2025 and positive mid single digits in Q2 2026, and the footprint is still 21 states.
  • Capital returns compound. Roughly $263M of free cash flow a year, a net-debt-light balance sheet, and $437M left on the buyback authorization at fiscal year end.

What a bear points to

  • Comps keep slipping. Q2 2026 was −0.4% after +2.9% in Q1; four of the last six quarters were negative.
  • Margin flattered by one-offs. Q2 2026 gross margin of 40.4% included tariff refunds, much of it reinvested below the line, and full-year net income guidance was left unchanged.
  • The core customer is stretched. Management repeatedly flags pressure on lower-income households, and the stock is up about 60% from its 52-week low.

Catalysts to watch

  • Q3 2026 results (early December): whether comps hold near the guided back-half ~+1%, and early holiday trends.
  • Fiscal 2027 guidance (March 2027): the store-opening cadence and whether margin guidance holds without tariff refunds.
  • Buyback pace: with the stock at a 52-week high, slower repurchases would be a signal on how management sees value.

Risks to the call

  • Upside: a consumer rebound. In this model, +1pt of comps on its own adds only about $1.70 per share; the value is in what comps do to margin. Spreading rent and payroll over more sales is where the money is: each +0.5pt of operating margin adds about $5.70.
  • Downside: new-store productivity fades as the chain moves into markets with less brand awareness, or tariffs return without offsetting price.
  • Model risk: about 70% of the value sits in the terminal value, so the discount rate dominates. See the sensitivity table.
